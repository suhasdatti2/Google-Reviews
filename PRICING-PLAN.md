## 3. Which options get "Best Value"

- **Counter Cards → "Full Counter" (3 cards, $64.99).** Lowest per-card price ($21.66) and it maxes out the counter setup.
- **Bulk Cards → "High Volume" (50 cards, $174.99).** This is the one you *want* most restaurants to buy — enough to matter, not so many it feels like a commitment. Give it the visual highlight.
- The 100-pack gets **"Lowest Per Card"** instead of "Best Value" — that keeps "Best Value" pointing at the package you actually want to move (the 50), while still rewarding the big spender.

## 4. Which is the main featured offer

**Flagship product: the Premium Counter Card.** It's what the brand leads with — highest margin, easiest to explain, and it's the "hero" photo on the homepage.

**Featured package inside it: "Twin Station" ($49.99), labeled Most Popular** and visually highlighted. Two cards feels like the responsible choice (one for backup / a second station) and it lifts your average order from $29.99 to $49.99 without much resistance.

So the store funnel is: lead with the Counter Card → highlight the Twin Station → then offer Bulk Handout Cards as the volume upsell for restaurants and shops that want to hand cards out.

## 6. Simple profit logic

Plug in your real supplier numbers — these are conservative example costs (blank printed NFC cards run roughly $1–2 each premium, $0.40–0.60 each in bulk). Programming is basically free (a few seconds per card with your phone).

**Assumed costs:** premium card ~$1.50 · bulk card ~$0.40–0.55 · shipping $4–12 · payment fees ~2.9% + $0.30.

**Premium Counter Cards**
| Package | Price | Est. cost | Est. profit | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solo Station | $29.99 | ~$6.70 | ~$23.30 | ~78% |
| Twin Station | $49.99 | ~$9.25 | ~$40.75 | ~81% |
| Full Counter | $64.99 | ~$11.70 | ~$53.30 | ~82% |

**Bulk Handout Cards**
| Package | Price | Est. cost | Est. profit | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter (10) | $49.99 | ~$13.25 | ~$36.75 | ~73% |
| Busy Shop (25) | $99.99 | ~$23.70 | ~$76.30 | ~76% |
| High Volume (50) | $174.99 | ~$37.90 | ~$137.10 | ~78% |
| Max Pack (100) | $299.99 | ~$61.00 | ~$239.00 | ~80% |

**The takeaway:** even if your true card cost is *double* my estimate, every package still clears ~65% margin. The rules that keep it healthy:
1. Never let per-card price drop below ~$2.50 on bulk (your 100-pack at $3.00 protects this).
2. Bundle shipping into the price mentally — the counter packs barely cost more to ship than a single card, so multi-packs are almost pure margin.
3. Bigger packs = higher profit *per order*, even at lower margin %. Push customers up the ladder.

## 9. Why bulk cards are useful (for restaurants)

Use this on the Bulk product page and in your pitch:

> **Your counter card is great — but most of your customers never reach the counter.** They order takeout, grab the bag, and leave. They pay at the table and walk out. The single card by the register never gets in their hands.
>
> Bulk Handout Cards fix that. Drop one in every takeout bag. Tuck one under the check. Clip one to the receipt. Now the review card goes home *with* the customer, while the meal is still fresh in their mind — and leaving a review is one tap, no searching, no app.
>
> More cards in more hands means more chances for happy customers to actually leave the review they meant to. At $3–5 a card with no monthly fee, one new regular from one good review pays for the whole box.

## 10. Sounding affordable but premium

- **Anchor with the premium card first**, then bulk feels like a deal. Seeing $29.99 for one nice card makes $3.50 for a bulk card read as a steal.
- **Say "one-time" and "no monthly fee" everywhere.** The absence of a subscription is your premium *and* your affordability story at once.
- **Price per card on bulk, total price on counter.** "$3.00 each" feels cheap; "$29.99" feels like a real product, not a throwaway.
- **Keep the .99 endings** — they read as retail/affordable. Don't round to $30 / $175; it makes it feel more expensive without adding value.
- **Premium comes from the words and the look, not a high price:** "glossy black," "matte white print," "programmed and tap-tested before it ships," and the clean black-and-white store. The design carries the premium so the price doesn't have to be high.
- **Never discount the counter card below $29.99.** If you want a promo, bundle (add cards) rather than cut the price — protects the premium feel.
